# Visual and Prompt Specification

Use this reference to turn a photograph into a selectively edited, front-facing paper shadow box. Adapt it to the actual source instead of copying every clause mechanically.

## 1. Memory edit

Identify these before choosing planes:

- **Emotional center:** what the remembered moment is about.
- **Identification anchor:** one compact subject or landmark that must remain recognizable.
- **Dominant spatial gesture:** the main sweep, axis, framing device, or overlap.
- **Quiet field:** sky, water, pavement, wall, snow, sand, darkness, or another broad area that can be expanded.
- **Deletions:** incidental people, crowds, vehicles, signage, repeated windows, clutter, minor buildings, small foliage, and other objects that do not support the memory.

The source supplies facts and relationships, not an obligation to preserve every item. A successful reinterpretation can simplify position, scale, shape, and count while retaining the anchor and mood.

## 2. Depth selection

Rank candidate regions using occlusion, scale, frame position, focus, atmospheric contrast, semantic importance, and silhouette value. Select 4–6 planes with a clear reason for every plane; prefer five strong planes over six weak ones.

Use the plane stack to organize large masses, not to reproduce every depth discontinuity. Multiple simplified objects may share one plane when they function as one visual group.

## 3. Shape language

- Make the image read first as 3–6 oversized blunt masses and quiet negative space.
- Use naive, slightly misproportioned geometry with chunky irregular silhouettes and soft hand-cut wobble.
- Compress vegetation into 1–3 tonal clusters and crowds into zero to two representative faceless figure groups.
- Reduce buildings and geology to planes; keep only a few decisive rooflines, windows, railings, or cutouts needed for recognition.
- Remove fragile gaps, repeated grooves, leaf detail, masonry, anatomy, fabric folds, and decorative filler.
- Avoid isolated floating fragments unless visibly attached or intentionally supported.

## 4. Faceless figure rule

Every human figure, regardless of size or importance, must be faceless. Use blank paper ovals, flat profile blocks, hair masses, hats, or solid silhouettes. Do not generate eyes, eyebrows, noses, nostrils, mouths, lips, teeth, facial highlights, portraits, or realistic skin texture. Convey identity only through pose, clothing color, hairstyle mass, scale, and relationship to the scene.

## 5. Color and material

- Derive 5–8 broad matte color families from the photograph.
- Favor dusty midtones, restrained contrast, one or two source-derived accents, and sparse near-black.
- Use opaque gouache/cut-paper/riso/screen-print-like fills with fine uniform paper tooth, slight density variation, and occasional gentle misregistration.
- Keep the planes physically constructed from matte fibrous cardstock with consistent edge thickness.
- Use flat colored shadow shapes where helpful, but no smooth gradients, photorealistic texture, wet watercolor effects, or glossy materials.

## 6. Frontal construction

- Show the shadow box in strict front elevation, camera centered and parallel to the art.
- Keep all outer frame edges parallel to the image edges. No visible left, right, top, or bottom side wall; no three-quarter view or receding perspective.
- Reveal depth through shadows inside silhouette cutouts, narrow separation between overlapping planes, fine exposed paper rims, and consistent soft shadows cast backward.
- A restrained frame may surround the scene, but the illustration should dominate and the frame should remain symmetrical.

## Prompt skeleton

> Using the supplied photograph as source memory rather than an exact layout, selectively reinterpret the scene as one handcrafted [N]-layer paper shadow box viewed perfectly straight on. Preserve [IDENTIFICATION ANCHOR] through its silhouette, signature colors, position, and relationship to [SUPPORTING FORM], while omitting [DELETIONS]. Retain only [FIGURE COUNT/GROUPS]; remove the remaining crowd and incidental people. Front-to-back planes: [LAYER STACK]. Recompose the scene into 3–6 oversized blunt masses plus a broad quiet field of [SKY/WATER/GROUND/WALL]. Use gently naive cartoon-like hand-drawn shapes, chunky irregular hand-cut contours, simplified geometry, and 5–8 dusty matte color families derived from the source. Compress vegetation, architecture, and background detail into a few decisive planes. Render every person as a compact faceless paper figure with no eyes, eyebrows, nose, mouth, or facial detail. Construct the image from matte fibrous cardstock with visible cut edges, narrow layer spacing, contact shadows, and consistent soft shadows falling onto the planes behind. Camera centered and parallel to the artwork; strict front elevation; no visible side of the box and no perspective skew. Preserve the source aspect ratio. No text, watermark, photographic patches, smooth gradients, polished vector art, or unrelated objects.

Add only source-specific features needed to preserve recognition. Do not add sticker columns, keywords, journaling-card furniture, or other layout elements unless the user explicitly requests them.

## Example pruning patterns

- Crowded fountain: near railing → one or two representative pairs → fountain → simplified tree/building mass → dusk sky; omit all other spectators and cars.
- Travel portrait: one foreground framing mass → faceless main figure or pair → landmark → simplified distant terrain → sky; omit passersby and minor structures.
- Street scene: one near sign/curb mass → one representative person or vehicle → simplified storefront block → distant building mass → sky/quiet wall; remove advertising and repeated pedestrians.
- Temple courtyard: near foliage or statue → zero to two visitor pairs → courtyard plane → one balustrade plane → simplified temple facade/backdrop; omit the rest of the crowd and architectural repetition.

These are editing patterns, not mandatory content. Never invent an object solely to complete a pattern.
